# Evidence card: what controlled tests exist

Contents: [How to use this card](#how-to-use-this-card) · [Opened tests](#opened-tests) · [Not opened](#not-opened-no-figures-stated) · [Our methods](#our-methods) · [Answer template](#answer-template-offered-on-request)

Reference for the question "does astrology work?". Offer it when the person asks, not unprompted. Every row cites the
repo's research record (`research/ledger/findings.json`, finding id) and says whether that source was actually opened
(`accessed`). Figures appear only for opened sources and are the sources' own, as audited in that ledger; none is
page-checked by the owner. Readings stay interpretive, never certainties.

## How to use this card

- State a figure only from a row with `accessed: true`, and name the study. A row with `accessed: false` carries no figure.
- Never cite Ertel's reanalysis, or the post-hoc professional subgroup in the ClearerThinking study, as validation.
- Say "no controlled test located" for Vedic dasha or transit timing and for Lal Kitab. That is a search result,
  not "refuted" and not validated.
- A client saying "that fits me" is not evidence (Barnum effect, EVID-F5).

## Opened tests

| Study | What was tested | Result (as reported) | Locator | accessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carlson, Nature 318 (1985) | Western astrologers matching natal charts to personality profiles, double-blind, success level agreed beforehand | 40 of 116 first choices correct against the agreed 50%; chance about one third. Confidence did not track accuracy | EVID-F1; Table 2 p.423 | true |
| Narlikar et al., Current Science 96(5) (2009) | Indian astrologers telling two student groups apart from charts, double-blind | 27 of 51 registered astrologers returned answers; mean 17.25 of 40 against chance 20; best 24 against a threshold of 28 | EVID-F3; pp.641-643 | true |
| Nanninga, Astrotest, Correlation 15(2) (1996/97) | 44 astrologers matching 7 charts to 7 questionnaires | Mean 0.75 correct against 1.0 expected by chance; 22 of 44 got none | EVID-F7 (audited "weakened" only on minor design details); Skepsis reprint | true |
| Dean and Kelly, J. Consciousness Studies 10(6-7) (2003) | Time twins; two meta-analyses (author-hosted copy) | Time twins: no relation found. Astrologer agreement: mean effect size 0.10. Matching studies: 0.05, not significant | EVID-F5; the critique of the time-twin test was not opened | true |
| Mars effect, via Nienhuys, Skeptical Inquirer 21(6) (1997) | Gauquelin's champions-and-Mars claim, retested under the French CFEPP protocol | Claimed about 22% against about 17% base rate; CFEPP 18.66% (209 of 1,120) against 17.70% by random coupling. Reported through one partisan secondary source | EVID-F6 (Benski et al. 1996 itself not opened) | true (secondary only) |
| ClearerThinking (Greenberg and Ferretti, 2024) | 152 astrologers picking the real chart from 5 candidates, 12 cases | Mean 2.49 of 12 against chance 2.4. Not peer-reviewed; Western charts only. Its post-hoc subgroup of 17 professionals scored higher; the authors call that hard to interpret, so it is a hypothesis, not evidence | EVID-F8 | true |

## Our methods

- Vedic dasha and transit timing, Lal Kitab predictions and remedies, varshaphal, muhurta and Ashtakoota matching: no
  controlled test located (EVID-F9; two web searches, not an exhaustive literature search).
- The tests above are Western personality matching, or single-factor rules, or extreme-group discrimination. They do not
  test consultation synthesis, and a null there does not show the skill's methods fail. It does show that a chart reading
  has not been shown to beat chance.
- The way to learn more is prospective: the prediction log (`astro predict`) records a hit criterion and a baseline
  before the outcome is known.
