What this skill is

A Vedic astrology consultation for Claude. It has three parts: a local calculation engine (positions, dashas, transits, strengths), method files that say how to reason from those facts, and a cited book library behind the rules.

Facts are computed. Interpretation follows documented methods. Nothing is sent to an outside service. This is not legal or financial advice, and astrology's predictive validity is not scientifically established. Health and death are not read in this practice. Such predictions can harm the reader and are not good for their health. I can help with another area of your life instead.

Install and first use

In Claude Code:

claude plugin marketplace add Krish9688/vedic-astrology-consultation
claude plugin install astrology-consultation@vedic-astrology

Then run /astrology-consultation:setup in a session and /reload-plugins. Setup builds a private Python environment and downloads the ephemeris files. It needs Python 3.11+ or uv, and internet once.

No Claude? Use the engine alone:

git clone https://github.com/Krish9688/vedic-astrology-consultation && cd vedic-astrology-consultation
./install.sh        # needs uv; add --all for PDF reports (WeasyPrint needs Pango)
.venv/bin/astro doctor

Then ask, for example: “Calculate my chart.” Platforms: macOS and Linux (Windows via WSL 2).

Asking questions

Ask in plain chat. Give the birth date, birth time and birth place, and say where the time came from (birth certificate, family memory, a rectified time). The source matters because a few minutes can change the rising sign.

You get a short, life-first answer:

  • what it means for you and its likely form
  • a grade for how clearly the chart agrees
  • timing as periods
  • a counter-factor: what pulls the other way
  • what to watch

How to read a report

Grades (Strong, Moderate, Weak) say how clearly the chart agrees with itself on a point. They do not say whether the area of life is good or bad.

Windows and phases are stretches of time when a theme is active. Read them as weather, not verdicts.

The appendix “Why each conclusion” lists the evidence behind every statement, so you can check it.

Birth-time sensitivity shows which statements survive a few minutes of error. If it says high, treat that statement as uncertain.

The knowledge graph

The rules come from a library of 51 books, held as a graph of 32,186 nodes and 280,374 edges. Each rule points to the page that supports it.

Node: a thing (a book, a page, a rule, a planet); the number under its name is how many exist. Edge: an arrow from one node to another; the number is how many exist. Colour: sage = sources parchment, dashed = claims mint = concepts.

The graph is built deterministically from page-cited rule maps. It never merges rules, and it keeps disagreements as Contradiction nodes. The public edition ships the tools, not the private library graph.

What it does well

  • Positions and dates are computed and checked against the engine, never recalled.
  • Every claim is graded: Strong, Moderate, Weak, Contradictory or Unknown.
  • Rules are page-cited, so a reader can look the source up.
  • Contradictions are kept, not smoothed over.
  • Safety limits are built in (see Safety and privacy).

Known limits

An independent review by two reviewers of a full Kundli report found these problems. They are listed so you can read reports with them in mind.

  • Grade words collide with ordinary adjectives, so “strong” can be misread.
  • Most grades land on Moderate, which limits how well they separate strong points from weak ones.
  • Phase notes inherit the grade of their window instead of being graded on their own.
  • A planet that rules a hard house is framed only by its natural meaning, not by that functional role.
  • A transit date can be printed without an evidence file behind it.
  • Western books are in the library but are never used for Vedic readings.
  • There is no muhurta (choosing a date) module.
  • PDF output needs WeasyPrint; without it, print the HTML report from a browser.
  • Some report types are still planned.

What you can learn

  • How to check a claim's source in the appendix, and judge whether it supports the claim.

Safety and privacy

  • Health and death are not read in this practice. Such predictions can harm the reader and are not good for their health. I can help with another area of your life instead.
  • Remedies are Parashari only: no gems, no costly rituals, and every remedy carries a line saying no efficacy is claimed.
  • No verdicts on other people.
  • Your data stays on your computer.

Glossary

Lagna
The rising sign at birth; it sets the houses.
Dasha
A planetary period in a fixed sequence that times life themes.
Antardasha
A sub-period inside a dasha.
Sade Sati
Saturn's roughly seven-and-a-half-year passage around the Moon sign.
Shadbala
A six-fold measure of a planet's strength.
Ashtakavarga
A points system showing how supportive each sign is.
Navamsa (D9)
The ninth-part chart; it refines a planet's real strength.
Karaka
A planet that signifies a theme, such as Venus for relationships.
Yoga
A named combination of planets that points to a result.
